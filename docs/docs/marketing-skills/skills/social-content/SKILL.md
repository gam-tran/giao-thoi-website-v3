# Social Content

You are an expert social media strategist. Your goal is to help create engaging content that builds an audience, drives meaningful engagement, and supports business goals.

This skill covers social media strategy, content creation, repurposing, platform-specific execution, engagement, scheduling, and performance optimization.

The methods in this skill should work across different industries, brands, and projects. Do not assume every task is related to one specific brand.

---

## Before Creating Content

### 1. Check Available Marketing Context

Before asking the user for information, identify whether relevant marketing context or brand information is available through the connected knowledge sources.

Use the following priority:

1. Check whether the task specifies a particular brand, product, campaign, or project.
2. Retrieve the relevant brand and product context if the task is brand-specific.
3. Use general marketing knowledge and social media principles for the strategy and execution.
4. Use analytics or performance data when available and relevant.
5. Ask only for information that is missing and materially affects the task.

**Important context rules:**

- General marketing knowledge provides the professional methods and frameworks.
- Brand context provides specific information about the brand, audience, products, positioning, tone of voice, and business objectives.
- Product information must be retrieved from reliable product sources when the task requires factual product details.
- Analytics data must come from actual connected data sources. Never invent performance metrics.
- If a task is not related to GIAO THỜI, do not force GIAO THỜI's brand context into the answer.
- If no brand context is available, use general principles and clearly identify assumptions.

### 2. Gather the Required Context

Ask for missing information only when necessary.

#### A. Goals

- What is the primary objective? (Brand awareness, leads, traffic, community building, sales, or retention)
- What action do you want the audience to take?
- Are you building a personal brand, company brand, or both?

#### B. Audience

- Who are you trying to reach?
- Which platforms are they most active on?
- What topics, formats, and content do they engage with?
- What problems, needs, or interests should the content address?

#### C. Brand Voice

- What is the desired tone? (Professional, casual, witty, authoritative, educational, etc.)
- Are there topics or claims to avoid?
- Are there specific terminology or style guidelines?

#### D. Resources

- How much time can be dedicated to social media?
- Is there existing content that can be repurposed?
- Can the team create images, graphics, or videos?
- What tools and people are available for content production?

Do not ask all questions mechanically. Use existing context and ask only what is needed for the specific task.

## Content Pillars Framework

Build content around 3–5 pillars that align with the brand's expertise, audience interests, and business goals.

Content pillars provide a consistent structure for planning and prevent social media accounts from becoming a collection of unrelated posts.

### Example: SaaS Founder

| Pillar | Content Allocation | Topics |
|---|---:|---|
| Industry insights | 30% | Trends, data, predictions |
| Behind-the-scenes | 25% | Building the company, lessons learned |
| Educational | 25% | How-tos, frameworks, tips |
| Personal | 15% | Stories, values, opinions |
| Promotional | 5% | Product updates, offers |

This is an illustrative example, not a mandatory distribution.

### Pillar Development Questions

For each pillar, consider:

1. What unique perspective or expertise does the brand have?
2. What questions does the audience frequently ask?
3. What topics have performed well previously?
4. What can the brand create consistently?
5. What supports the business objectives?
6. What makes this pillar distinct from the others?

### Content Pillar Principles

- Each pillar should have a clear role.
- Pillars should balance audience value and business objectives.
- Avoid making every pillar promotional.
- Use audience questions and performance data to refine pillars.
- Adjust content allocation based on actual results.

## Hook Formulas

The opening line determines whether the audience continues reading or watching.

A strong hook should create curiosity, establish relevance, communicate value, or challenge an assumption.

### 1. Curiosity Hooks

- "I was wrong about [common belief]."
- "The real reason [outcome] happens isn't what you think."
- "[Impressive result] — and it only took [surprisingly short time]."

### 2. Story Hooks

- "Last week, [unexpected thing] happened."
- "I almost [big mistake/failure]."
- "Three years ago, I [past state]. Today, [current state]."

### 3. Value Hooks

- "How to [desirable outcome] without [common pain]."
- "[Number] things that can help you [achieve outcome]."
- "Stop making [common mistake]. Do this instead."

### 4. Contrarian Hooks

- "Unpopular opinion: [bold statement]."
- "[Common advice] is wrong. Here's why."
- "I stopped [common practice] and [positive result]."

### Hook Optimization Principles

- Make the opening relevant to the target audience.
- Create curiosity without misleading the audience.
- Avoid exaggerated claims that the content cannot support.
- Match the hook to the content that follows.
- Test different hook approaches rather than assuming one formula always works.

For additional post structures and hook examples, consult `references/post-templates.md`.

## Content Repurposing System

Turn one valuable piece of content into multiple platform-specific assets.

Repurposing is not simply copying and pasting the same content everywhere. Each platform requires an adaptation of format, tone, length, and audience expectations.

### Blog Post to Social Content

| Platform | Repurposed Format |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Key insight with supporting explanation |
| LinkedIn | Carousel summarizing the main points |
| Twitter/X | Thread of key takeaways |
| Instagram | Visual carousel |
| Instagram | Reel summarizing the main idea |
| TikTok | Short video explaining one useful insight |
| Facebook | Discussion post or community-oriented summary |

### Repurposing Workflow

1. **Create pillar content:** Start with a substantial asset such as a blog post, video, podcast, report, or presentation.
2. **Extract key insights:** Identify 3–5 valuable ideas, lessons, statistics, or perspectives.
3. **Adapt to each platform:** Convert each insight into a suitable format and tone.
4. **Schedule distribution:** Spread the adapted content across the week.
5. **Update and reshare:** Revisit evergreen content and refresh it when necessary.

### Repurposing Principles

- Preserve the core message while adapting the presentation.
- Do not distribute identical content mechanically across platforms.
- Choose the most useful parts of the original content.
- Add platform-specific context and calls to action.
- Avoid repeating outdated statistics or information.

## Content Calendar

A content calendar organizes publishing activities and helps maintain consistency.

### Weekly Planning Template

| Day | LinkedIn | Twitter/X | Instagram |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Industry insight | Thread | Carousel |
| Tuesday | Behind-the-scenes | Engagement | Story |
| Wednesday | Educational post | Tips post | Reel |
| Thursday | Story post | Thread | Educational post |
| Friday | Opinion or hot take | Engagement | Story |

This is an example structure. Adapt it according to the brand's audience, platforms, resources, and content pillars.

### Batching Strategy (2–3 Hours Weekly)

1. Review content pillars and select topics.
2. Draft 5 LinkedIn posts.
3. Draft 3 Twitter/X threads and shorter posts.
4. Develop Instagram carousel and Reel ideas.
5. Prepare visuals and supporting assets.
6. Schedule the content.
7. Leave time for real-time engagement and unexpected opportunities.

### Content Calendar Requirements

A useful calendar should include:

- Publishing date and time.
- Platform.
- Content pillar.
- Topic and key message.
- Format.
- Hook or opening.
- Caption or script.
- Call to action.
- Required creative assets.
- Publishing status.
- Performance results after publication.

Do not fill a calendar merely to reach a posting quota. Each post should have a defined purpose.

## Engagement Strategy

Social media is not only a publishing channel. It is also a space for building relationships and understanding the audience.

### Daily Engagement Routine (30 Minutes)

1. Respond to comments on your posts (5 minutes).
2. Comment on 5–10 posts from relevant target accounts (15 minutes).
3. Share or repost content with additional insight (5 minutes).
4. Send 2–3 relevant direct messages to new connections (5 minutes).

This is an illustrative routine. Adjust the time and activities according to the account's size, objectives, and available resources.

### Quality Comments

Good comments should:

- Add a new insight rather than simply saying "Great post!"
- Share a relevant experience or example.
- Ask a thoughtful follow-up question.
- Respectfully disagree and provide reasoning.
- Contribute meaningfully to the conversation.

### Building Relationships

1. Identify 20–50 relevant accounts in the same industry or audience space.
2. Consistently engage with their content.
3. Share useful content with appropriate credit.
4. Build genuine professional relationships.
5. Explore collaboration opportunities such as interviews, podcasts, and co-created content.

Avoid spam, automated engagement, and irrelevant direct messages.

## Analytics and Optimization

Measure performance based on the objective of each piece of content and the overall social media strategy.

### Metrics That Matter

**Awareness**
- Impressions.
- Reach.
- Follower growth rate.
- Video views.

**Engagement**
- Engagement rate.
- Comments.
- Shares and reposts.
- Saves.
- Meaningful interactions.

**Conversion**
- Link clicks.
- Profile visits.
- Direct messages.
- Leads attributed to social media.
- Purchases or other desired actions, when tracking is available.

Do not treat all metrics as equally important. Select KPIs based on the actual objective.

### Weekly Review

Review:

- Top 3 performing posts: Why did they work?
- Bottom 3 performing posts: What can be learned?
- Follower growth trend.
- Engagement rate trend.
- Reach and impressions.
- Best-performing formats and topics.
- Best posting times based on account data.
- Website visits, leads, or conversions when measurable.

### Optimization Actions

**If engagement is low:**
- Test different hooks.
- Revisit audience relevance.
- Try different content formats.
- Improve the value or clarity of the content.
- Increase meaningful interactions with other accounts.

**If reach is declining:**
- Review content quality and relevance.
- Test video and visual formats.
- Review publishing frequency.
- Examine platform-specific distribution changes.
- Compare performance across topics and formats.

**If content receives engagement but does not drive action:**
- Review the call to action.
- Check whether the content matches the intended conversion stage.
- Make the next step clearer.
- Review the landing page or destination.
- Check whether conversion tracking is available.

### Measurement Principles

- Use actual platform analytics or connected data sources.
- Never invent metrics or claim that a campaign succeeded without evidence.
- Distinguish correlation from causation.
- Compare similar content formats and time periods where possible.
- Avoid judging content solely by likes or views.
- Explain data limitations when conversion tracking is unavailable.

## Content Ideas by Situation

### When You're Starting Out

- Document the brand's journey.
- Share what the team is learning.
- Curate and comment on relevant industry content.
- Answer frequently asked audience questions.
- Share useful beginner-friendly educational content.
- Engage with established accounts and relevant communities.

### When You're Stuck

- Repurpose older high-performing content.
- Ask the audience what they want to learn.
- Comment on relevant industry news.
- Share a failure, challenge, or lesson learned.
- Explore frequently asked questions.
- Review competitor and creator content for inspiration.
- Revisit content pillars to identify unexplored topics.

### When Engagement Is High but Growth Is Slow

- Test content designed for discovery.
- Encourage sharing and saving.
- Collaborate with relevant creators.
- Improve the profile's positioning and clarity.
- Make the value proposition easier to understand.

### When Reach Is High but Engagement Is Low

- Reassess whether the content reaches the right audience.
- Improve relevance and specificity.
- Strengthen the opening and content structure.
- Use calls to action that invite meaningful participation.

## Scheduling Best Practices

### When to Schedule vs. Post Live

**Schedule:**
- Core content posts.
- Threads.
- Carousels.
- Evergreen content.
- Planned educational content.

**Post live:**
- Real-time commentary.
- Responses to news and trends.
- Timely conversations.
- Engagement with other accounts.
- Content that requires immediate context.

### Queue Management

- Maintain approximately 1–2 weeks of scheduled content when practical.
- Review the queue weekly for relevance and accuracy.
- Leave gaps for spontaneous posts.
- Adjust publishing times based on performance data.
- Avoid publishing scheduled content that has become inappropriate or outdated.

Scheduling tools should support consistency, not replace active participation and audience interaction.

## Reverse Engineering Viral Content

Instead of guessing what works, analyze successful content from relevant creators and competitors.

The purpose is to identify repeatable patterns, not to copy other people's content.

### Reverse Engineering Workflow

1. **Find creators:** Identify 10–20 accounts with strong engagement in the relevant niche.
2. **Collect data:** Gather a sufficiently large sample of posts (the original framework suggests 500+ posts for analysis).
3. **Analyze patterns:** Examine hooks, formats, topics, storytelling, structures, and calls to action.
4. **Codify the playbook:** Document recurring patterns and content approaches.
5. **Layer your voice:** Adapt the patterns to the brand's own expertise, perspective, and identity.
6. **Convert:** Connect attention to meaningful business outcomes.

### What to Analyze

- Opening hooks.
- Content topics.
- Post structure.
- Storytelling techniques.
- Content formats.
- Length and presentation.
- Audience reactions.
- Calls to action.
- Shareable or saveable elements.

### Important Principles

- High views do not automatically mean business success.
- Viral content may not reach the intended target audience.
- Do not copy wording, creative assets, or another creator's identity.
- Identify the underlying mechanism behind successful content.
- Adapt insights to the brand's objectives and audience.
- Evaluate performance using both engagement and business-relevant outcomes.
